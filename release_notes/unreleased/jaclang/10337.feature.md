- **Performance: faster one-character compares, number formatting and dict counting in native code**:
  - **One-character compares.** A comparison against a one-character literal, such as `c >= "a"` or `c == " "`, reads one byte instead of calling a string compare.
  - **Ints as text.** `str(int)`, an int's `repr`, and f-strings made of plain `{str}` and `{int}` pieces write their digits directly instead of going through `vsnprintf`.
  - **Floats as text.** `str(float)` finds the shortest repr directly for values with up to six decimals, and otherwise tries 15, 16 and 17 digits, instead of up to 17 rounds of `snprintf` and `strtod`. It still prints exactly what Python prints.
  - **Dict counting.** `d[k] = d.get(k, c) + e` hashes `k` and probes the dict once.

  `str(float)` runs 97% fewer instructions (0.46 s instead of 18 s for 20 M calls), f-strings 88% fewer, `str(int)` 73% fewer, and a tokenizer that scans characters 48% fewer.

- **New: `Autoscaler.get_runtime_status` answers why a scale target is where it is**: `assess_workload` reads the Deployment, which reports how many pods exist and never why, so a workload KEDA had idled because no trigger was firing and one sitting at zero because its scaler could not reach its metric endpoint were indistinguishable, and a silently broken scaler was invisible. `Autoscaler` also had no read method at all, so its own knowledge could not be asked for. It now exposes `get_runtime_status(scale_target_name, namespace, autoscaler_name=None)` returning an `AutoscalerRuntimeStatus` whose `state` reuses `ResourceStatus`, alongside `trigger_active`, `scaler_ready`, normalized `conditions` and the source `resourceVersion`. The KEDA engine combines the ScaledObject's `Ready` and `Active` conditions, the HPA KEDA generated (read by the name KEDA publishes, never rebuilt from a convention, because a failed external-metric fetch surfaces there while KEDA's own `Ready` can still read True) and the target workload; the HPA engine reports `ScalingActive` and leaves `trigger_active` as `None`, since a plain HPA has nothing that corresponds to a trigger firing. The caller passes a scale target rather than an autoscaler name, because the name depends on the scaling mode and deriving it was the guessing this abstraction exists to end. The live ScaledObject's `idleReplicaCount` is now the authority on the replica floor, so a Deployment applied before the `jac-scale.replica-floor` annotation existed is judged correctly instead of reading `degraded`, and a `ScaledObject` that is absent or denied by RBAC degrades to the workload's own state rather than reporting a scaler failure that was never observed.

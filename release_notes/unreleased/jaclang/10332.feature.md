- **Performance: copying a local at its last use moves its reference**: `a = b` between native locals retained the object for `a`, and `b` released its own reference later. The compiler moved the reference instead only when every use of `b` was such a copy. It now moves it whenever liveness shows that no path reads `b` after the copy, including when `b` is declared `Node | None`. The move doesn't happen inside a `try`, a lambda, a comprehension, a `flow` or a parallel loop, or when `b` is ever borrowed. A linked-list walk in the style of Richards' `append_to` runs 44% fewer instructions per node, and rotating three objects through a temporary 85% fewer.
